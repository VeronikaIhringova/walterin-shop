## 1. Brand Overview

**Brand:** Walterin
**Founder / artist:** Walter Ihring (Slovak illustrator)
**Website:** https://walterin.com
**What it is:** A storytelling brand built on two equal pillars — illustration and text. Walterin makes illustrated comic stories rooted in history, culture, and human experience, and turns them into physical objects (decks, books, prints, future products).
**Origin:** Walter's existing illustrated work — including the *Bratislava* travel-guide series (SK / DE editions, also published under the *Walterin* name) — is the artistic foundation the brand now builds on.
**Stage:** Pre-launch of first physical product (Comics Tarot of Consciousness). Launch target ~1 month from this document's date.

### What Walterin is *not*
- Not a generic tarot brand.
- Not a self-help / spirituality brand.
- Not a print-on-demand merch shop.
- Not a publisher of someone else's stories — every product is Walter's authored universe.

## 2. Strategic Positioning

### The core thesis
**Walterin turns story into objects you keep.** Every product is an illustrated narrative made physical — a deck, a book, a print — that rewards re-reading and re-looking.

### Positioning frame
- Mass-market tarot decks = mystical wallpaper, generic symbolism, interchangeable.
- Art-house decks = often beautiful but story-thin, treated as decor.
- **Walterin = illustrated comic story in deck form.** Each card is a panel. The deck reads like a graphic novel that can be shuffled.

### Core differentiators (ranked by uniqueness)
1. **Story-first illustration.** Walter is a comic illustrator, not a tarot designer adapting an aesthetic. The cards are scenes from a coherent universe, not symbol charts.
2. **Author identity.** A single voice and single hand behind every product. Not a curated collective, not a stock-art compilation.
3. **Bilingual native craft.** Copy and storytelling work natively in Slovak and English — the brand isn't a translated import; it's authored in both.
4. **Slovak origin, European sensibility.** Central-European literary atmosphere — neither Anglo-American nor Scandinavian — is itself a differentiator in a saturated market.
5. **Universe, not one-off.** The Tarot is the entry point to a planned product universe (future books, decks, prints). Buyers are signing up to a creator, not a single SKU.

### Primary — The Story-Led Collector
| Attribute | Profile |
|---|---|
| Age | 25–45 |
| Location | Slovakia + Czechia + DACH (existing reader base); EU + UK + US English-speaking design / illustration audiences |
| Behaviour | Buys illustrated books, indie comics, art-objects. Follows illustrators on Instagram, supports small Kickstarters. |
| Motivation | Wants beautifully made objects with a story behind them. Allergic to generic. |

### Secondary — The Walter Ihring Reader
Existing readership of Walter's *Bratislava* and forthcoming city books. Slovak/Czech and German-speaking readers who already bought into his illustrated voice. They're the easiest first-buyer cohort and the basis for the launch lift.

### Tertiary — The Tarot-Curious Aesthete
Buyers who land on the deck because they like the look but stay because of the universe. Reached via Instagram + Pinterest with the artwork doing the heavy lifting.

### Audiences we are explicitly *not* targeting
- Hardcore esoteric / occult tarot communities (we won't optimize copy or imagery for them).
- Self-help / wellness buyers looking for "healing tools".
- Volume-discount mass-market gift buyers.

## 5. Brand Voice & Personality

### Archetype
**The Storyteller.** Bold, warm, literary, intentional. A voice that has read books and watched cinema, that knows when to whisper and when to make a clean strong statement. Slovak literary sensibility softened by humor; never academic, never corporate.

### Voice rules — DO
- Lead with image and story. Set a scene, name a feeling, then stop.
- Use rhythm — short sentence, longer sentence, breath.
- Be specific: a city, a year, a face, a fragment of a myth.
- Treat copy as a companion to the artwork, not a label for it.
- Match the atmosphere of the card or page being shown — cinematic for a wide scene, intimate for a close one.
- Mix Slovak and English with intent. When publishing in one language, write *natively* in that language.

### Voice rules — DON'T
- Generic spiritual / mystical language ("the universe is guiding you", "tap into your inner wisdom").
- Marketing clichés ("must-have", "elevate", "transform your life").
- Spoiler-heavy descriptions of cards or stories. Tease, don't summarize.
- Corporate hedging ("we believe", "we strive to").
- Hashtag soup. Hashtags should feel like signposts, not seasoning.
- Treat translations as throwaway — never publish copy that reads as Google-translated.

### Value propositions
- **For collectors:** "An illustrated story you can shuffle. A deck made by a single hand, from a single universe."
- **For Walter's existing readers:** "The next chapter of the same world. New format, same voice."
- **For first-time buyers:** "Tarot you'd want on the shelf even if you never read a card."

### Typography

- **Companion:** Cormorant Garamond — literary / editorial sections, longer-form copy, story passages.
- **Body / UI:** TBD (a workhorse sans for site UI to avoid over-using Cormorant for small text).
- Don't introduce a third display family without a clear reason.

### Tuck box anatomy (Comics Tarot of Consciousness)
- **Top of front face:** Walterin logo
- **Center of front face:** "COMICS TAROT" set large (Walterin Bold)
- **Subordinated below:** "of Consciousness" — quieter weight, smaller scale, clearly secondary
- **Hero illustration on front face:** The Magician card
- **Back panel:** Brand red box / panel — the signature graphic mark of the deck

### Photography & illustration direction
- The illustration is the hero. Photography of products should never compete with the artwork.
- For lifestyle shots: low ambient light, warm tones, suggestion of a reader / collector environment (a desk, a book, a coffee, a window) — never staged crystal-and-incense tarot tropes.
- For card-reveal posts: flat scans + selective close-ups that emphasize linework and atmosphere.

### Layout principles
- Generous whitespace; quiet around the artwork.
- Editorial, not e-commerce-default. Treat product pages like exhibition wall labels.
- Restraint in motion — minimal animation; let the illustrations carry the energy.

## 10. Critical Context for AI Assistants

1. **Walterin is a brand, not just a deck.** When writing copy or strategy, default to the brand's universe framing whenever it strengthens the work.
2. **Product framing rule:** Comics Tarot of Consciousness is a product — never describe it through Jungian, psychological, or therapy language.
3. **Two-language native rule:** Slovak and English copy must each feel like it was written, not translated. When in doubt, write each language separately rather than translating.
4. **Tone calibration:** bold, warm, literary, never generic. Every line must earn its place.
5. **Walter has strong personal preferences.** When a creative suggestion conflicts with proven market-effective practice, flag the tension and explain both sides — don't quietly choose.
6. **Ship over polish, but never sloppy.** First print run is small (~150). Every customer is effectively a brand ambassador. Treat early copy and packaging touches like they'll be photographed.
7. **The artwork is the lead.** Copy is the companion. Don't overwrite the image.
8. **No spoilers.** Captions and product copy should tease the universe — not summarize cards or stories.
9. **Default to "universe" thinking.** Today's piece of work should compound into next month's launch and next year's catalog.
10. **Veronka is the bridge.** Collaborate. Propose. Invent. Flag conflicts. Don't stop and ask when a creative call would do.
