# COPY GUIDE

> How every Walterin text is written, in every language, on every surface: product pages, emails,
> captions, packaging, ads. Standing rule from Veronka, 23 Sep 2026.
> Facts: docs/WALTERIN-GROUND-TRUTH.md and docs/products/*.md. Voice: ground truth §7.

## The rule

1. **Write the way a person speaks.** If nobody would say it out loud, rewrite it.
2. **No metaphors for their own sake.** No "look inwards", no self-help or marketing register.
3. **Concrete before abstract.** Name the thing, the person, the moment.
4. **Nothing repeats between sections.** Each one has its own job.
5. **Slovak is written natively**, never translated from English. Same for every other language.
6. **Read it aloud in your head before showing it.** Cut whatever sounds written rather than said.

## How to check a draft

- Read the first sentence out loud. If you stumble or feel silly, it's wrong.
- Find every abstract noun ("awareness", "journey", "experience") and try the concrete version.
- Put the accordions or sections next to each other. If two say the same thing, one of them loses it.
- Slovak: would you say this to a friend in a café? "Pre niekoho, kto sa rád pozerá dovnútra" — no.
  "Darček pre niekoho blízkeho" — yes.
- Check the facts against the ground truth before the phrasing: a beautiful sentence with the
  wrong number is still wrong.

## 7. Break the lines by meaning — but write paragraphs, not poems

Where the lines break changes how a sentence sounds. A break in the wrong place makes good copy
read badly, and too many breaks turn prose into verse.

The full rule is [LINE-BREAKS.md](LINE-BREAKS.md). In short:

- A short **lead statement stays on one line**. It is the sentence that has to land.
- **Body text is a paragraph.** Lines of a similar, fairly long length that fill the column. Do not
  give every clause its own line. A paragraph wrapping mid-phrase is correct.
- **Related short sentences share a line.** `Cities become symbols. Facts become scenes.`
- **The ending may have its own line**, for emphasis. Once per paragraph, at most.
- **Never strand a single word from the next sentence** at a line end — `…sharpest form. Five`.
  A short group of words carried over is fine.
- Keep pairs together with a no-break space: `From €18,99`, `9:00–17:00`.
- **Check the phone as well as the desktop.** The same paragraph breaks differently at 390px.

This is rule 6 continued. Where you breathe when you read it aloud is where the line ends — and you
do not breathe after every clause.

## What this rules out

Marketing register ("elevate", "must-have", "unlock", "discover the power of"), therapy register
("healing journey", "your inner self"), invented certainty (dates, specs, claims nobody confirmed),
and any sentence whose job is to sound good rather than to say something.

## The reference texts (Veronka, 8 October 2026)

**When you are unsure how something should sound, read these three first.** They are the home
page's expandable texts and they are the standard for every piece of copy on this site, in both
languages.

### Short stories

**EN** Every story is a nine-frame comic on a single page, so you read it in a few minutes. You’ll
learn something you’ve probably never heard before. And since one page is one story, you can start
anywhere.

**SK** Každý príbeh je vyrozprávaný v deviatich krokoch na jednej strane, takže ho prečítate za pár
minút. Dozviete sa niečo, o čom ste asi ešte nikdy nepočuli. A keďže jedna strana je jeden príbeh,
začať môžete kdekoľvek.

### Witty history

**EN** Everything is drawn with wit and precision. Whether it’s a famous figure or a well-known
place, you’ll smile, and the drawing stays with you. History suddenly feels fresh.

**SK** Všetko je nakreslené vtipne a výstižne. Či ide o slávnu osobnosť, alebo známe miesto, vždy sa
pousmejete a kresba vám ostane v pamäti. História tak zrazu pôsobí sviežo.

### A universal language

**EN** Comics are a language everyone understands, young and old. The drawings tell the story on
their own, so you’ll enjoy it even if you don’t know much about history.

**SK** Komiks je jazyk, ktorému rozumie každý, mladší aj starší. Kresby hovoria samy za seba, takže
si príbeh užijete, aj keď o histórii veľa neviete.

### What makes them the standard

1. **Say what the reader gets.** Every sentence is something that happens to the person reading:
   you read it in a few minutes, you learn something, you smile, you can start anywhere. Not what
   the work is — what it does for them.
2. **Warm and concrete.** A nine-frame comic. A single page. A famous figure or a well-known place.
   Nouns you can point at.
3. **No abstract lines.** Nothing like *"where observation meets nuance"* or *"ideas travel
   lightly"*. If a sentence could sit under any product in the world, it is not ours.
4. **No repetition between the three.** Each answers a different question — how long does it take,
   what does it feel like, do I need to know anything first. Nothing is said twice.
5. **No school theme.** We are not teaching and the reader is not studying. "You'll learn something
   you've probably never heard before" is a gift, not a lesson.
6. **In Slovak: "v deviatich krokoch."** Not *"v deviatich políčkach"* and not a literal rendering
   of "nine-frame". This is the agreed phrase and it stays.

**Slovak is written, not translated.** The two versions above say the same thing and are not the
same sentences — compare *"the drawing stays with you"* with *"kresba vám ostane v pamäti"*. Draft
each language on its own, then check they promise the same thing.
