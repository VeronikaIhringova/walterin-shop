Illustration and text.
Neither one is the decoration.

Most illustrated books pick a side. The picture explains the sentence, or the sentence explains the picture. Walterin books are built the other way, and that is the whole brand in one line.

The drawing is not the illustration of the text

There is no caption telling you the goalkeeper lost. The panel already told you. The words in a Walterin book do a different job — they say the thing the picture cannot: a date, a name, the sentence somebody actually said in 1968.

The text is not the caption of the drawing

Walter writes his books before he finishes drawing them. The research is real — streets, archives, the awkward bits other guidebooks leave out. Then the drawing decides what gets cut, which is usually half of it.

Nine frames, because that is how long a thought is

Short enough for a tram ride, long enough for a turn. It is the unit the whole universe is built from: the city books, the deck, whatever comes next.

Who makes them

Walter Ihring

Born in Bratislava, 1969. Thirty-five years of ink, a Golden Gander for caricature in 2016, three books about cities and a deck of 78 cards going to print in three weeks. Usually in a café, drawing whoever sits down opposite.
