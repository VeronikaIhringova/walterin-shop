S2 · How a card is read

Three directions, mocked up with the real artwork and the measured nine-frame grid. Every frame you see enlarged is cropped from the card image itself — no new files. The two readings sit below their own rule in all three. Open a mock-up and click the frames; arrow keys step, Esc stops the walk.

1 · The reading rail

The text leads. Three row blocks, nine lines, the card small beside them with the active frame ringed and enlarged underneath. Read it with me walks the three rows — seven seconds each — with a Stop button.

1440 · frame 5 open
390 · Slovak
Why it works
  • The lesson is the text, so it reads top to bottom like a page — the card stops competing with it.
  • All nine captions are visible at once, so the three rows are obvious before you click anything.
  • Cheapest to build and the most robust: plain buttons and a ring, no drawn geometry.
What it costs
  • The card is a map, not a picture — the artwork is at its smallest here.
  • Nine uppercase lines in one column is a lot of the same shape.
  • The enlarged frame sits under the card, away from the line you clicked.

2 · The annotated print

The card sits whole and small in the middle of the paper. The chosen frame is enlarged in a note in the margin, with an ink arrow drawn from the note to the frame. Notes alternate left and right, as if someone annotated a print. Back / Next, nine dots in three groups.

1440 · frame 5, note on the left
390 · card and note side by side
Why it works
  • It looks like Walterin: hand-drawn arrow, paper, a note in the margin. The most brand-specific of the three.
  • One frame at a time, enlarged, with its own caption — the clearest single-frame explanation.
  • Shortest section of the three: the whole teaching part ends 720px down at 1440.
What it costs
  • You only ever see one frame explained; the nine rows are told, not shown.
  • The arrow is measured geometry — it has to be redrawn on every resize and every step.
  • Nine steps is nine clicks for anyone who wants the whole card.

3 · The storyboard

The nine frames are lifted out of the card and laid out as they sit on it: three labelled rows, each frame enlarged with its caption beside it. The whole card stays in the corner as a map. Nothing has to be clicked for the section to be understood.

1440 · the whole board
390 · the board keeps its 3 × 3 shape
Why it works
  • The only direction that teaches without interaction — it reads the same to a scroller and to a clicker.
  • The rows are headings with the frames underneath, so the three-row structure is the layout itself.
  • Same shape on the phone, so nothing has to be re-learned between devices.
What it costs
  • The tallest of the three; at 1440 the two readings start at 880px — one screen, but only just.
  • Nine crops on screen at once is a lot of yellow; the card as an object disappears.
  • Each frame is small (96px) — the printed captions inside them are not legible, only the typeset ones.
