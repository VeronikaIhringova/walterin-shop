S2 · The annotated print — three ways to link the note to the frame

Everything that worked is untouched: the card beside the note, the enlarged frame with its explanation, the dimming and its opacity, Back / Next, "try another card". What changes in all three: the connector is redrawn from scratch, the three rows carry the deck's own colours, and the card picker sits somewhere different in each one so you can compare. Open a version and step through with the buttons or the arrow keys — the connector is measured off the lit frame, so it lands on it at any width.

Taken from the card files themselves, not chosen: these are the three most-used background colours in the deck. Note that the deck's "green" is a turquoise — there is no leafy green in the set.

The colour appears in three places at once: the note's header band, the ring around the enlarged frame in the note, and the nine step dots, grouped three by three. That last one is what makes the structure readable at a glance — three coloured clusters, not nine identical dots.

1 · The docked note no connector

The note is pressed against the card like a bookmark pushed into it. A tail on the note's edge slides up and down to sit exactly on the lit frame's row — nothing is drawn across a gap, because there is no gap. The note always stays on the right. Picker: directly under the card, with the card's name.

1440 · the tail on frame 5
390
Why it works
  • Nothing can be badly drawn, because nothing is drawn: the link is the contact itself.
  • The calmest of the three — the eye goes card → frame → note with no line to follow.
  • The tail is one transform, so it stays exact at every width, including the phone.
What it costs
  • The note never changes sides, so the layout is always the same shape.
  • The tail marks the row, not the single frame — the ring does the rest of the work.
  • The picker under the card pushes the section about 60px taller.

2 · The highlighter run marker stroke

A marker stroke in the row's colour sweeps out of the note and stops where the frame begins, with a fine pen line through it so it stays visible where it crosses the card. It draws itself in 300ms. The note alternates sides. Picker: on the control bar, with Back / Next — one row for everything you can do.

1440 · the stroke ends on the frame's edge
390
Why it works
  • It looks like someone marked the card by hand — the closest of the three to how the deck is drawn.
  • The colour is doing two jobs: it links the note to the frame and it names the row.
  • Every control sits on one bar, so the section ends 770px down at 1440 — the shortest of the three.
What it costs
  • It is still a line across a gap: it has to be recomputed on every step and every resize.
  • Over a yellow card the yellow row needs the pen line to stay visible — the colour alone isn't enough.
  • The picker on the control bar is busier than it looks in the screenshot once the names appear on hover.

3 · The dotted trail and the hand manicule

Ink dots run from the note and end in a pointing hand — the printer's manicule, in the deck's own white glove with an ink outline and a cuff in the row's colour. The fingertip sits five pixels off the lit frame. When the gap is too short for dots, the hand points on its own. Picker: up in the header, under the intro, before the card.

1440 · the hand on frame 5
390
Why it works
  • A manicule is exactly what an annotated print has in the margin — the idea and the form agree.
  • The white glove is already in the artwork, so the hand belongs to the deck rather than to the website.
  • It points at one frame, unmistakably, and it is the most memorable of the three.
What it costs
  • The hand sits on top of the artwork for the middle and right columns — it covers a dimmed frame.
  • Most drawing to maintain: a hand, a trail, and the rule for when the trail is dropped.
  • The picker in the header pushes the card down; on the phone the note ends at 820px, close to the fold.
