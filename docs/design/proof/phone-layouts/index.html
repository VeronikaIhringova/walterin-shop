The shopping row on phones

Decided 7 October 2026: B — one card per row. A, C and D are gone from the theme, along with the ?grid= switch that compared them; this page stays as the record of how the choice was made. Also decided that day: equal card heights on a desk so the bottoms line up, natural heights on phones where a row holds one card anyway.

Four layouts, same build, same moment. Safari (WebKit), real phone widths with touch emulated, so the pills behave as they do on a phone and not as they do in a narrow desktop window. Pick a width, a language and a page; the four options stay side by side.

What changed in all four

  • The name leads. It was the other way round: the title was 17px at weight 500 and the price 16px at weight 700 — a pixel smaller and two weights heavier, so the eye landed on the price. Now the name is 16px on a narrow card and 18px on a wide one, and the price steps down to 13px / 15px at weight 500.
  • The price keeps full ink. Greying it is the easy way to make it recede, but it was invisible yellow until this morning and is not going back towards low contrast to make a point about hierarchy. Size and weight carry it; contrast stays 15.77:1.
  • No dead space under the price. Three separate things were adding it, found one box at a time: this file's own padding, a 10px foot on Dawn's information grid, and the price wrapper's line-height strut hanging 6px below the number it holds. The space under the price is now exactly the padding — 12px on a narrow card, 14px on a wide one — and nothing else.
  • One gap between name and price, the same on every card: 6px when they stack, 24px between the columns when they sit side by side.
  • Slimmer pills. 34px tall at 12px rather than 44px at 13px, and the bar now hugs its words instead of spanning the card — stretched across a full-width card, a pill holding "Coming soon" in the middle of 340px reads as an empty input field.
One decision is yours, and it is not a detail. Cards no longer stretch to match the tallest in the row. Equal heights and "no empty areas" cannot both be had: the stretch has to go somewhere, and it goes under the price of the shorter titles as a block of card colour with nothing in it — which is the empty area in your screenshot. This was first written as a phone-only rule, on the reasoning that a desk shows every title on two lines anyway. Measuring proved that wrong: 36px and 57px of dead space at 768 and 1024, and 36px at 1440 in Slovak, where Komiksový tarot vedomia runs to three lines and the English runs to two. So it was never a phone problem — it was a long-title problem, and Slovak is the longer language. The cost is that cards in a row no longer end on one line. The images still do. If you prefer the ruled bottom edge, the rule comes out and the empty area comes back.

How they differ

  • A · two per row. Closest to now. With three products the last card sits alone beside an empty half-row, and at 390 the tarot title needs four lines — the narrowest column makes the longest name work hardest.
  • B · one per row. The artwork is roughly four times the area. Every title fits on two lines, the name and price sit side by side on one baseline, and the date fits the pill on one line. Three products is exactly the number this suits; it would get long at eight.
  • C · swipe row. Keeps the section short and signals "there is more" with the next card peeking. The cost is that products two and three are never seen by someone who does not swipe — and on a shop with three products, hiding two of them is a real price to pay.
  • D · 1 + 2. The only layout with no half-empty row. The lead card carries the product being pushed, the two below are the rest of the shop. It also builds in an editorial decision: something has to be first.

Still true

  • Card colour #FFFEFB everywhere, no frame, pills always visible on phones and hover-revealed on a desk.
  • Nothing here is live. Preview only, and the ?grid= switch and its snippet get deleted once you pick one.
