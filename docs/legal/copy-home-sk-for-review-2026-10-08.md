# The three expandable texts — EN and SK side by side

**Built and in the preview. The Slovak is a draft for Veronka to correct**; the English is her
approved 30 September copy and is unchanged apart from heading case.

Written natively, not translated: where the literal Slovak would have been stiff, the sentence was
rewritten to say the same thing the way a person says it. Every departure is noted.

---

## 1 · Short stories · **Krátke príbehy**

**Preview**

> **EN** History, in its sharpest form. Five minutes or fifty, enter anywhere.
> **SK** História v skratke, ale ostrá. Päť minút či päťdesiat — začnite kdekoľvek.

**Full**

> **EN** History, in its sharpest form. Five minutes or fifty, enter anywhere. Each story unfolds in
> nine frames: precise, distilled, alive. Made for the spaces in between. And for moments you'd
> rather not rush.
>
> **SK** História v skratke, ale ostrá. Päť minút či päťdesiat — začnite kdekoľvek. Každý príbeh má
> deväť políčok: presný, zhustený, živý. Pre chvíle medzi dvoma zastávkami. A pre tie, ktoré sa
> neoplatí uponáhľať.

**Departures from the literal**

- *"in its sharpest form"* → **"v skratke, ale ostrá"**. The literal *"v najostrejšej podobe"* is a
  construction nobody says out loud. "In brief, but sharp" keeps the contrast and sounds spoken.
- *"nine frames"* → **"deväť políčok"**. `políčko` is what a comic panel is actually called in
  Slovak; `okienko` would read as a little window.
- *"Made for the spaces in between"* → **"Pre chvíle medzi dvoma zastávkami"** — *for the moments
  between two stops*. The English abstraction becomes a tram ride, which is where these are read.

## 2 · Witty history · **Vtipná história**

**Preview**

> **EN** Sharp insights. Clean lines. A point of view.
> **SK** Ostré postrehy. Čisté linky. Jasný uhol pohľadu.

**Full**

> **EN** Sharp insights. Clean lines. A point of view. History told with timing, where observation
> meets nuance, and a single detail can tilt the whole perspective. The kind of knowledge that
> lingers longer than the page.
>
> **SK** Ostré postrehy. Čisté linky. Jasný uhol pohľadu. História rozprávaná s citom pre
> načasovanie, kde jediný detail dokáže obrátiť celý pohľad. Poznanie, ktoré vydrží dlhšie než
> stránka.

**Departures from the literal**

- *"where observation meets nuance"* is **dropped**. In Slovak it becomes two abstract nouns
  shaking hands and says nothing; the sentence is stronger without it, and "a single detail can
  tilt the whole perspective" already carries the idea.
- *"told with timing"* → **"s citom pre načasovanie"** — *with a feel for timing*. The bare
  *"s načasovaním"* reads like a train schedule.

## 3 · A universal language · **Univerzálny jazyk**

**Preview**

> **EN** Comics move where language hesitates.
> **SK** Komiks ide tam, kde jazyk zaváha.

**Full**

> **EN** Comics move where language hesitates. Cities become symbols. Facts become scenes. Ideas
> travel lightly, but stay with you. This is history you don't just read. You recognize it.
>
> **SK** Komiks ide tam, kde jazyk zaváha. Z miest sa stávajú symboly, z faktov scény. Myšlienky
> cestujú naľahko, a aj tak vám zostanú. Toto nie je história, ktorú len čítate. Spoznávate ju.

**Departures from the literal**

- *"Cities become symbols. Facts become scenes."* → **one sentence**, *"Z miest sa stávajú symboly,
  z faktov scény."* Slovak lets the second verb drop, and keeping two short sentences here would
  have been the "one clause per line" rhythm the line-break rules forbid.
- *"but stay with you"* → **"a aj tak vám zostanú"** — *and stay with you all the same*. The plain
  *"ale zostanú"* loses the small surprise the English "but" is doing.
- *"You recognize it"* → **"Spoznávate ju."** Kept as its own short sentence: it is the line the
  whole paragraph is walking towards, and the rules allow the ending a line of its own, once.
